How this was measured

  • linear is upstream NeuralAmpModelerCore at 0b3d3c9. Its FFT path uses power-of-two tiers that grow along the impulse response, from a 128-tap direct head up to 2048-tap partitions at 8192 taps.
  • linearplus is the same commit plus one change, 79c8009. Its FFT path uses uniform partitions, 256 taps long up to 2048 taps and 512 up to 8192, with the partition multiplies spread across the callbacks between transforms.
  • Both convolve directly up to 1024 taps, with the same code, so those columns measure the same thing twice. A difference of 0.1 there is measurement noise.
  • Both run as each would in a plugin, choosing direct or FFT convolution by themselves. They were measured in one process by nam_ir_benchmark, taking turns at each point on the same generated mono IR and the same 10.9 s guitar DI.
  • Every callback is timed. The p99 is taken over all callbacks from the passes the protocol accepted, which are the tightest 70% of passes. The mean is the mean of those passes, per callback.
  • Before anything is timed, both outputs are rendered and compared, and a point where they disagree is not reported.
  • The multi‑second tables (48,000 to 2,880,000 taps, 1 to 60 s) were run separately. Past 8192 taps linear uses a 64‑tap head with partitions up to 4096 (to 48,000 taps) and 8192 beyond. linearplus uses 1024‑tap partitions to 48,000 taps; past that, uniform partitions cover the first 2 × T taps and a tail tier of T‑tap partitions the rest, with T 8192 up to 240,000 taps and 16384 beyond. The tail's transforms are split into transforms of the uniform tier's own size and spread, with its multiplies, across the samples between its blocks, so no callback does a large transform.
  • At those lengths linear's slowest callbacks are not its transforms. Every 32 × the declared maximum block it copies its whole input history, the full length of the IR, back to the start of its buffer: 11.5 MB at 60 s. The tables use NAMBench's usual declaration, a maximum equal to the callback, so that copy lands in one callback in 32. A host may declare a larger maximum than it sends; the hover tables add linear@4096, the same code told to expect up to 4096 frames, which makes the copy 4096 / callback times rarer. That lowers its mean and p99 but not the cost of the callback that makes the copy. linearplus keeps only its head's history, so the declared maximum makes no difference to it. The hover tables also give the worst callback and the rate of missed deadlines, and ⚠ marks a cell where any of them missed one.
  • On the M2 a few of linearplus's multi‑second cells are marked too: at most 13 late callbacks in over three million, the worst taking 49 ms against a p99 of 20 µs. Nothing in the code takes that long, and the Pi, which runs pinned to one core with nothing else on it, has no such callbacks, so these are the machine stalling the process rather than the convolution.
